France: Au-delà de Paris

Discovering the soul of the Republic beyond the capital

Lyon

The Gastronomic Capital of France
Landmark: Fourvière Hill
Cuisine: Le Coq au Vin
🏛️ HistoryLyon was founded by the Romans in 43 BC and became one of the most important cities in ancient Gaul. It later developed into a major center for silk production during the Renaissance.
🍽️ CuisineLyon is called the gastronomic capital of France. Popular dishes include Coq au Vin and Quenelles, served in traditional restaurants called bouchons.

Nice

Jewel of the French Riviera
Landmark: Promenade des Anglais
Cuisine: Salade Niçoise
🏛️ HistoryNice has a rich history influenced by both France and Italy. It was part of Italy until 1860, after which it became a part of France.
🍽️ CuisineThe cuisine is fresh and Mediterranean-based. Famous dishes include Salade Niçoise and Ratatouille.

Marseille

The Mediterranean Gateway
Landmark: The Old Port
Cuisine: Bouillabaisse
🏛️ HistoryMarseille is the oldest city in France, founded by Greek sailors around 600 BC. It has always been a major port city connecting France to the world.
🍽️ CuisineThe city is famous for seafood dishes, especially Bouillabaisse, a traditional fish stew using olive oil and fresh herbs.

Bordeaux

City of Art and Elegance
Landmark: Place de la Bourse
Cuisine: Canelés
🏛️ HistoryBordeaux has a long history as a major trading port, especially during the 18th century when it became wealthy through maritime commerce.
🍽️ CuisineBordeaux is world-famous for its historic vineyards and caramelized Canelé pastries.
