Discovering the soul of the Republic beyond the capital

Lyon

The Gastronomic Capital of France
Landmark: Fourvière Hill
Cuisine: Le Coq au Vin
🏛️ History Lyon was founded by the Romans in 43 BC and became one of the most important cities in ancient Gaul. It later developed into a major center for silk production during the Renaissance, making it economically and culturally significant.
🎭 Culture Lyon is known as the cultural heart of France, with museums, theatres, and historical architecture. The city is famous for its "traboules," unique hidden passageways that weave through the Old Town's buildings.
🍽️ Cuisine Lyon is called the gastronomic capital of France. Popular dishes include Coq au Vin and Quenelles, served in traditional restaurants called bouchons. It is a place where culinary tradition meets modern Michelin-starred innovation.
📍 Attractions Key must-visit spots include the Basilica of Notre-Dame de Fourvière, the cobblestone streets of Old Lyon, the vast Parc de la Tête d'Or, and the majestic Lyon Cathedral located by the river.

Nice

Jewel of the French Riviera
Landmark: Promenade des Anglais
Cuisine: Salade Niçoise
🏛️ History Nice has a rich history influenced by both France and Italy. It was part of Italy until 1860, after which it became a part of France. Its strategic location on the Mediterranean coast made it an important trade and cultural center for centuries.
🎭 Culture Nice reflects a vibrant Mediterranean lifestyle with art, music, and festivals. It is world-famous for its connection to artists like Henri Matisse and its colorful old town filled with narrow, bustling streets.
🍽️ Cuisine The cuisine is fresh and Mediterranean-based. Famous dishes include Salade Niçoise, Ratatouille, and various seafood specialties. Street food like Socca is also a local staple you cannot miss.
📍 Attractions Famous sites include the Promenade des Anglais, the historic Old Town (Vieux Nice), the panoramic views from Castle Hill, and the Marc Chagall National Museum.

Marseille

The Mediterranean Gateway
Landmark: The Old Port
Cuisine: Bouillabaisse
🏛️ History Marseille is the oldest city in France, founded by Greek sailors around 600 BC. It has always been a major port city, connecting France with different parts of the world and acting as a bridge between Europe and Africa.
🎭 Culture Marseille is known for its diverse and multicultural population. The city blends traditional French culture with strong Mediterranean influences, creating a gritty and energetic urban atmosphere.
🍽️ Cuisine The city is famous for seafood dishes, especially Bouillabaisse, a traditional and complex fish stew. Olive oil, fresh herbs, and the daily catch from the port are essential components of their cooking.
📍 Attractions Key landmarks include the Old Port (Vieux-Port), the stunning Notre-Dame de la Garde, the turquoise waters of Calanques National Park, and the modern MuCEM Museum.

Bordeaux

City of Art and Elegance
Landmark: Place de la Bourse
Cuisine: Canelés
🏛️ History Bordeaux has a long history as a major trading port, especially during the 18th century. It became incredibly wealthy through global trade and maritime commerce, which funded its beautiful limestone architecture.
🎭 Culture Bordeaux is known for its elegant architecture and relaxed, high-end lifestyle. It is a UNESCO World Heritage city, perfectly reflecting classical French culture and sophisticated urban design.
🍽️ Cuisine Bordeaux is world-famous for its historic vineyards. Local culinary specialties include the caramelized Canelé pastries, Entrecôte à la Bordelaise (steak in wine sauce), and fine regional cheeses.
📍 Attractions Don't miss the Place de la Bourse and its Water Mirror, the Bordeaux Cathedral, the futuristic Wine Museum (La Cité du Vin), and the scenic Garonne Riverfront.
